    The 6 C’s

    The details

    Where to stand
    Your man…Where you standSay
    Has the ballArm’s length, between him and the rim. Square to him, chest in front.“Ball!”
    One pass awayIn the gap: a third to halfway up the line to the ball, a step or two off it. Point at man and ball.“Help left!” or “Help right!”
    Two passes away, ball above the free-throw lineOn the lane line. Open stance, see both.“Help!”
    Two passes away, ball below the free-throw lineIn the middle of the key. See both.“Help!”

    In the gap, stay level with your man and keep your chest in the gap until you take a charge, the ball is passed, or he picks up his dribble. Then recover with a cross step.

    On the ball
    • Pressure at arm’s length and keep him in front.
    • Watch his chest, not the ball or his eyes.
    • Wall off drives with your chest and an inside arm bar. Outside hand up, so the referee sees a clean wall.
    • No reaching or hand-checking. Most steals come off the ball.
    • Dead dribble: jam his pivot foot and yell “Kill! Kill!” Everyone else jumps into the passing lanes.
    • After he passes, jump to the ball right away.
    Cutters
    Cut toward the ball (flash)Bump him with an arm bar. Never let him cut in front of you.
    Cut to the basket (give-and-go)Jump to the ball first, so he has to cut behind you. Keep him in sight.
    Cut away from the basketJump to the ball, keep him in sight, then find your help-line spot.
    Help and rotation
    • Low I (the lowest help defender) meets a drive outside the paint, even a baseline drive.
    • High I (the next helper up) drops to protect the rim.
    • Everyone else sinks and splits the two closest shooters.
    • Back-door cuts only happen when we stop jumping to the ball.
    Screens
    ScreenWhat we doSay
    Off-ball screenGo through. The screener’s defender pulls toward the ball and guards a man and a half until you’re back.“Screen left!” or “Screen right!”
    Cutter screened toward the basketBump him and recover.“Bump!”
    Ball screenHedge and over: the big steps out level with the screen so the dribbler goes wide. The guard gets skinny and goes over. The big sprints back with high hands.Big: “Hedge, hedge, hedge!” Guard: “Back, back, back!”
    Ball screen, same sizeSwitch with contact, no gap.“Switch!”
    Dribble handoffGo one under.“Under!”
    Posts
    • Low post, ball above the free-throw line: three-quarter front on the high side.
    • High post (elbow): front on the low side.
    • If the entry pass gets in: drop behind him and go straight up, elbows to ears.
    • Guards dig at the post from the ball side.
    Transition
    • When we shoot, the two guards head back. The other three crash, then sprint back.
    • Never jam the rebounder or jump the outlet pass. Get back.
    • Sprint to the paint first. No buddy running. Basket first, then the ball, then your man.
    • Get ahead of the ball line.
    Rebounding: one and done
    • All five rebound on every shot. Nobody leaks out.
    • Assume every shot misses.
    • Side shots miss to the far side about 7 times in 10. Straight-on shots come back toward the shooter about 7 times in 10.
    • Front pivot if he’s a step or two away; reverse pivot if he’s right on you. Hold him about 3 seconds.
    • Box out about 6 feet from the rim (farther on 3s). Three of us make a triangle around the rim; a fourth takes the long rebounds.
    • Go get it with two hands, chin it, and outlet to the sideline.

    The 70% rule: a shot from the side misses to the far side about 7 times in 10. A shot from straight on comes back toward the shooter about 7 times in 10.

      Five zone rules

      The details

      Our spots
      WhoSpotMain job
      1 and 2 (guards)The two spots up topGuard the ball up top and on the wings. Take away the high post.
      3 and 4 (forwards)The low spots, one each sideGuard the corners and short corners. Close out to a wing when a guard can’t get there.
      5 (centre)The middle of the keyStay between the ball and the basket. Front the ball-side post. Guard the high post if it gets there.
      Ball on the wing and in the corner
      • Ball on the wing: the guard on that side takes it and pushes it toward the baseline. The other guard takes the high post. The ball-side forward drops to the short corner. 5 slides to the ball-side block. The weak-side forward steps up the lane.
      • Ball in the corner: the forward steps out and guards it. 5 fronts the post. The guard sinks into the lane. The other guard takes the high post.
      • Back up the floor: everyone goes back to the spots for the new ball.
      Skip passes and bumps
      • On a skip pass, everyone slides while the ball is in the air.
      • If no guard can get to the wing in time, the forward closes out right away to stop the shot.
      • The guard sprints over and calls “Bump!” The forward drops back to his low spot.
      High post and short corner
      • Best plan: the high post never gets the ball. Guards keep a hand in the passing lane.
      • If it gets in: 5 guards it, 3 and 4 pinch in toward the rim, and the guards squeeze in so his only pass is back out.
      • Ball in the short corner: we trap it every time. The forward and 5 trap; the other forward guards the paint; one guard blocks the pass back out and the other looks to steal.
      Drives
      • A drive is stopped by the next player in the zone. No straight-line drives.
      • Baseline drive past a forward: 5 slides over and stops it before the block, and the weak-side forward protects the rim.
      • Once he passes out, everyone sprints back to the spots for the new ball.
      Cutters and handoffs
      • A cutter through the zone gets bumped and called: “Cutter!”
      • Pass him off to the teammate whose area he goes into, then go back to your spot.
      • Handoffs and players switching spots in front of the zone: we switch.
      Rebounding in the zone
      • In a zone you don’t have one man. On the shot, find the nearest body in your area and box out.
      • A side shot misses to the far side about 7 times in 10, so the weak side is ours.
      • Two hands, chin it, outlet to the sideline.

      Six rules

      The details

      The basic action
      JobWhoWhat he does
      Down screenerThe passer at the topSprints to screen about one step below the far elbow, on the edge of the lane, flat to the baseline. Feet wide, arms in, make contact.
      Butt cutterThe far wingCuts hard off the screener’s back to the rim, then fills the far corner.
      Long cutterThe far cornerWalks his man in a few steps, then sprints off the screen to the top.

      After the cuts, the screener flares to the wing. That puts us back in 5-out, ready to reverse it and go again.

      Butt cutter’s reads
      • Curl: the normal cut, off the screener’s back to the rim.
      • Back cut: his man jams the cut or gets caught high. Cut behind him before you reach the screen.
      • L cut: his man goes under the screen to meet you. Plant and pop to the top. The screener slips and the long cutter L cuts to the wing.
      • Naked cut: sprint straight to the rim before the screener even gets there.
      Screener’s and long cutter’s reads
      • Screener slips: when his defender gets above the screen, he plants, snaps his head around and dives to the rim. Both cutters L cut.
      • Screener’s naked face cut: he cuts in front of his man to the rim without screening. Both cutters L cut.
      • Long cutter curls: his man trails the screen, so he curls tight and catches going downhill.
      • Long cutter fades: his man cuts under the screen, so he fades a step for the shot.
      Who has to react?
      The readThe other two cutters
      Butt cutter L cutsScreener slips to the rim, long cutter L cuts to the wing
      Screener slips or face cutsButt cutter L cuts to the top, long cutter L cuts to the wing
      Back cut or naked cutNo change: run the normal circle

      The goal every time: one cutter at the rim, one on the weak-side wing, one at the top. Two cutters at the rim means one defender can guard both.

      Attack spots
      • The top and both wings are attack spots. If your man flies at you, drive and kick. Keep the ball moving.
      • On the reversal catch, drive right away while the defence is shifting, or don’t drive.
      • Against a sagging defence, look for the skip pass to the screener flaring to the wing.
      Ball to the corner
      • Pass to the corner, then cut hard to the block and post up.
      • Everyone fills up one spot. The player coming from the top to the wing uses an L cut.
      • Nothing inside? The cutter fills out to the far corner and we reverse it.
      Beating pressure
      • Denied on the wing? Don’t seal or juke. Back cut to the rim, or down screen for the corner. The corner fills the wing.
      • Dribbled at? Go back door. The next player fills up the sideline from behind.
      • Denied at the top? L cut toward half court to get open for the reversal.
      Passing and dribbling
      • Catch close to the 3-point line, square up and read. You can’t make these passes off the dribble.
      • Dribble only to attack the rim in a straight line, to make a better passing angle, or to dribble at a teammate.
      • Open teammate: pass into his shooting pocket. Defender on him: pass away from the defender. Never pass into the defence.
      • Use jump cuts to change speed and direction.

      Six rules

      The details

      Spots and the triangle
      SpotWhoJob
      Point1Top of the key. Picks a side and dribbles for a good passing angle. Swings the ball.
      Wings2 right, 3 leftStand low near the corner, in a line with 5. Look inside first.
      Middle5Middle of the key. Steps to the ball-side mid-post, just outside the key.
      Warrior4Lives on the baseline behind the zone. Slides to the ball-side short corner.

      Wing, 5 and 4 make a triangle around their big. He can’t guard both posts.

      Reading the triangle
      • Pass to 5: their big steps up, so 4 dives.
      • Pass to 4: their big goes to him, so 5 dives.
      • Big stays with one post: the other post is open.
      • Nobody comes out on the wing: shoot or drive the gap.
      • Their top guard drops to the wing: the top is open. Pass back to 1.
      Next options
      • Flash middle: 5 flashes into the middle of the zone, just below the free-throw line. Catch, face, read.
      • High-low: when their big steps up to 5 in the middle, 4 seals in behind him for the pass down.
      • Drive the gap: the wing attacks between two defenders. When the big steps up, dump it to the open post.
      • Step-out screen: 5 screens the wing’s defender, the wing drives the middle, 5 rolls to the rim.
      • Skip and seal: after a skip, their big is late. 5 ducks into the lane and pins him on his back. 4 runs the baseline.
      Having trouble getting open
      ProblemAnswer
      Wing is denied1 dribbles at him. The wing loops up to the top and 1 becomes the wing.
      Top is denied5 flashes to the high post. Swing it through him to the far wing.
      Wing gets trappedPivot away, keep it high. 5 flashes to the spot the trapper left.
      Their big covers both postsFlash 5 to the middle, or skip it and seal.
      Zone packs the paintSwing and skip it fast. Shoot the open shot, or drive the long closeout.
      Passing against a zone
      • Ball fake to move a defender, then pass where he was.
      • Pass to the hand away from the defender. Bounce passes get under the big’s hands.
      • Skip passes go over the top, chest high to the catcher. If the skip is being jumped, swing it through 1 instead.
      • Don’t dribble in front of the zone. Dribble only to get a passing angle or to drive a gap.
